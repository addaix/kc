# Ecosystem: prior art, external and internal

Prior art for `kc`, external and internal. Verdicts are one of *depend on it*, *wrap it*, *study it and move on*, or *avoid*. Licences were established from each project's LICENSE file rather than its package metadata, which disagree often enough to change a verdict.

## External ecosystem

142 entries were surveyed across nine clusters (about 125 distinct projects; Onyx, mem0, cognee, Khoj, RAGFlow, Morphik, R2R, `prov` and `rdflib` each appear in more than one). The verdict distribution is the finding, not the individual rows: **9 depend on it, 23 wrap it, 79 study it and move on, 31 avoid**. Nothing in the field is adoptable whole. Measured against `vd` + `ef` + `ir` + `raglab` + `contaix` + `lacing` + `py2mcp`, the overwhelming majority of what is on offer is duplicate retrieval inventory — mem0's 26 vector backends against `vd`'s ~15, Graphiti's RRF/MMR recipes against `ir`, everyone's chunk-and-embed against `ef` — bundled into an application that cannot be reduced to a component.

Two structural findings recur across unrelated clusters and should be read as corroboration rather than coincidence. First, **the paywall is drawn at ACL-mirroring ingestion**: Onyx put every `external_permissions/*/doc_sync.py` and its query-time censoring under a proprietary `ee/` licence while open-sourcing all 55 content connectors; SurfSense independently fenced its entire connector and crawler set behind BUSL; Nango's OAuth-lifecycle engine is ELv2; Mastra's brand-new enterprise carve-out runs straight through `auth/ee` (RBAC/FGA/ACL). Fetching content is commoditised and permissively available; knowing who may see it, and holding the credentials to keep asking, is neither. Second, **every project that bundles its own vector store also runs a paid cloud** — the bundling is the business model, not an engineering choice, and it is why none of these can be reduced to the component kc would want.

**Licence marking convention in the tables below:** a licence in **bold** is a hard blocker for commercialisable code (copyleft, source-available, non-commercial, or no grant at all). Every licence recorded here was read from the LICENSE file — and, where a PyPI/npm artifact exists, from inside the shipped artifact — never from the package metadata field. That discipline changed the answer in at least eleven cases.

The adoption evidence in this cluster is unusually clean and settles a design question: PROV has a decade's head start, W3C imprimatur and a complete formal ontology, and its flagship Python library has 138 stars against OpenLineage's 2,634 — roughly 19x uptake for the pragmatic JSON-facet subset over the standards-complete ontology. A small vocabulary wins; kc should own one. The single best idea here is `ColumnLineageDatasetFacet`'s **DIRECT vs INDIRECT** split — an input whose *values* flow into the output versus one that only determined which rows survived. Ported to kc that is the difference between a chunk that supplied the text of an answer and a chunk that merely influenced which chunks were retrieved, and none of `vd`/`ef`/`ir`/`raglab` encodes it.

Budget for tier 2.

The pre-filter question — "which of these N documents may this principal see" — has four implementable shapes, and the cluster splits cleanly along them: enumerate-then-intersect (safe only in SpiceDB), retrieve-then-check (composes with vector search but collapses k, and nobody supplies the over-fetch/backfill loop), compile-to-filter (Cerbos and OPA, but both are stateless and so cannot express ReBAC through a graph), and materialise (Leopard, Authzed Materialize — **no permissively-licensed implementation exists**). The raw material for the fourth does exist and is Apache-2.0: SpiceDB's `WatchService` and OpenFGA's `ReadChanges`. What neither gives you is the fan-out from "this tuple was written" to "these principal→document grants changed". That fan-out, plus the backfill loop, is kc's actual contribution here — and neither is retrieval, so nothing in the fleet overlaps it.

### The licence landscape

Reading the LICENSE file rather than the metadata field changed the answer eleven times, in both directions, and three of those would have put an unusable licence into a commercialisable product.

**Cleanly permissive, and safe to build on.** MIT: GEPA (zero dependencies), curies, docling, airbyte-python-cdk, LlamaIndex and its readers, DSPy, mcp-atlassian, `prov`, the MCP Python SDK, LangChain.js/LangGraph.js, EnterpriseRAG-Bench, R2R, LightRAG, Kuzu, AnythingLLM, LibreChat, GraphRAG. Apache-2.0: pySHACL, OpenLineage, ir_datasets, MTRAG, dlt, docling's ecosystem, Graphiti, mem0, cognee, MemMachine, SpiceDB, OpenFGA, Keto, Cerbos, OPA, Casbin, the MCP spec, SEP-2640, Agent Skills, llms.txt, A2A, Croissant, OASF, Vercel AI SDK, Haystack, txtai, PaperQA2, Ragas, TechQA, FreshStack code. BSD/W3C/dual: rdflib, NetworkX, pyld, hypothesis/h, pyoxigraph (MIT OR Apache-2.0), blake3 (CC0 OR Apache-2.0), OWL-RL, SPARQLWrapper. Two governance notes worth carrying: CNCF graduation (OPA) and CNCF hosting (OpenFGA) structurally preclude a BSL/SSPL relicense, and Casbin's move to the ASF is the strongest IP provenance in the survey.

**The AGPL landmines, and where they actually are.** The brief expected AGPL in the provenance and graph clusters and it is not there — I found exactly one GPL item (`wandexer`, zero stars) and no AGPL at all across OpenLineage, Marquez, DataHub, OpenMetadata, Atlas and `prov`. The AGPL is concentrated in two places instead. (1) **Applications with a paid cloud**: Basic Memory (AGPL-3.0-or-later, confirmed three ways) and Khoj (same, confirmed three ways). §13 fires on *network interaction*, not distribution, so a hosted kc linking either would owe complete corresponding source of the whole combined work to every user; there is no partial-use configuration that makes this safe, and neither should be read as a porting source. (2) **The Singer tap ecosystem**, which is booby-trapped: `singer-python` is Apache-2.0, the Meltano SDK is Apache-2.0, Meltano is MIT, most MeltanoLabs taps are Apache-2.0 — and every singer-io tap sampled is AGPL-3.0, *including* `MeltanoLabs/tap-salesforce` by fork lineage, so "use the Meltano fork" is not a mitigation. There is one hidden case that no metadata anywhere discloses: `pip install owlready2` (itself LGPL-3.0) silently places an **AGPL-3.0 Pellet reasoner** plus ~20 jars including log4j-core 2.19 in your tree, discoverable only by unpacking the sdist and reading `pellet/LICENSE.txt`.

**Source-available is the larger risk, and it is growing.** Seven blockers that are not copyleft but are equally unusable: Airbyte and Nango (byte-identical Elastic License 2.0, both reported `NOASSERTION` by GitHub); PyAirbyte (ELv2 while its PyPI package `airbyte` declares no licence at all); Morphik (BUSL-1.1 with a **US$2,000/month attributable-revenue cap** — a licence that terminates exactly when kc starts working); Memgraph (BSL 1.1 whose Additional Use Grant forbids embedding or distributing to third parties, i.e. precisely what a shipped kc does); SurfSense (BUSL-1.1 over `proprietary/`); OpenMetadata's shipped wheel (Collate CLA with a non-compete "Excluded Purpose" and automatic permanent termination on breach); and Mastra (Enterprise Edition License effective 2026-08-24, no time-based conversion). Add the licences that constrain rather than block: Open WebUI's branding clause above fifty users, Dify's prohibition on operating a multi-tenant environment plus a contributor clause allowing unilateral relicensing, CloudQuery's MPL-2.0, and the dataset side's CC BY-NC (CRAG, LoCoMo) and CC BY-SA (HotpotQA, FreshStack data). **Three of these are one to two years old or less**, and two of them (Open WebUI, Mastra) relicensed *from* more permissive terms — treat "it is Apache-2.0 today" on a VC-backed project as a statement about the current release only.

**The pattern that predicts all of it.** Every project in this survey that bundles its own vector store also runs a paid cloud: mem0, cognee, Zep, Basic Memory, memobase, MemMachine, txtai's platform, Morphik, SurfSense, Onyx, Dify, RAGFlow, Open WebUI, Mastra. The bundling is not an engineering decision, it is the moat — which is why "adopt the framework" and "keep the fleet's own storage layer" are mutually exclusive in practice, and why the component/application split in cluster 1 is the single most useful filter in the whole survey.

**Verification rules that earned their keep, in the order they mattered.** (1) Read the licence *inside the artifact you install* — OpenMetadata's repo is Apache-2.0 and its wheel is not, and this is the direction the usual "trust the LICENSE file over the metadata" rule gets backwards. (2) Read the LICENSE file, not the metadata field: PyPI declares no licence for cognee, `prov`, skosprovider, mcp-atlassian, `authzed`, `oso`, `multiformats` or PyAirbyte's `airbyte`; npm declares Apache-2.0 for `@mastra/core` while the tarball ships proprietary `ee/` binaries; Hugging Face tags MS MARCO `cc-by-sa-4.0` against Microsoft's written non-commercial terms; and `verba` on PyPI is a stranger's GPL-3 project on a name collision. (3) `NOASSERTION` means "read it yourself", not "permissive" — three of four `NOASSERTION` repos chased in the connector cluster turned out to be source-available, while for NetworkX, Quivr, blake3 and the MCP spec the same flag was a parser artefact over a perfectly permissive file. (4) Check the filename and the sub-paths: R2R's licence is at `LICENSE.md` (a raw `LICENSE` fetch 404s), opa-python-client's is `LICENCE.md`, Onyx's and SurfSense's and Mastra's boundaries are *directory paths* that a careless copy gets wrong, and anthropics/skills has no root LICENSE at all. (5) Check transitive closure: kglab wears MIT and hard-depends on GPL-2.0 `igraph`; the Neo4j driver is Apache-2.0 and the server it requires is GPL-3.0.

### What is actually worth taking

Nine things clear the bar of doing something none of `vd` / `ef` / `ir` / `raglab` / `contaix` / `lacing` / `py2mcp` already does. Six are components to install; the rest are designs to build, because no permissively-licensed implementation exists.

**Build (three, because nobody has).**

1. **The materialised principal→document permission index.** Zanzibar named it (Leopard) [12], Authzed sells it, OpenFGA's docs recommend it and implement nothing, and no permissive implementation exists anywhere. The Apache-2.0 raw material is there — SpiceDB's `WatchService` and OpenFGA's `ReadChanges` — but both stream *relationship* changes, not *computed permission* changes; the fan-out is kc's work. Two safety rules come free with it: thread a revision token from the engine through the index into the retrieval call (an index that lags the ACL store serves documents a user was just removed from), and own the over-fetch/backfill loop so an authorization-filtered k=10 returns ten results rather than three, silently, for the least-privileged users.

2. **The claim model: attributable *and* still true.** Four pieces, each seen in exactly one place and never together. Graphiti's bi-temporal `valid_at`/`invalid_at` with LLM-decided supersession; Mastra's `capturedAt` vs `when` ("we learned on Tuesday about something that happened in March") and its `maxScope` **promotion ceiling**, which is a sharper mechanism than a read-time ACL for letting a thread-level fact rise to org level while a confidential one never can; mem0's ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP reconciliation on the write path plus `history()` as a per-item change log; and OpenLineage's **DIRECT vs INDIRECT** distinction, which separates a chunk that supplied an answer's text from one that only influenced which chunks were retrieved. Only the last is permissively vendorable as a schema; the rest are designs to implement over `lacing`.

3. **Verifiable provenance.** SEP-2640's per-file SHA-256 digest manifest [14] and A2A's JWS-signed `AgentCardSignature` are the only integrity primitives in 142 entries, and nobody in the knowledge-layer space has combined them. "This claim is signed by the team that owns it, and its source bytes hash to this" is the literal middle word of kc's mandate and is entirely unclaimed. Pair it with a hard ingestion rule taken from the anthropics/skills failure: require an explicit licence on every item kc ingests and refuse to serve one that lacks it.

**Free-standing ideas worth an afternoon each, in rough order of payoff.** Hypothes.is's multi-selector re-anchoring (quoted text + character offsets + DOM range, tried in order of robustness) — `lacing` anchors on rational time, which is exact for media because the timeline does not move, but text moves and offsets rot silently. Onyx's MIT `ExternalAccess` vocabulary with `.empty()` as the documented **fail-closed** default, plus `ConnectorCheckpoint`, the `SlimDocument` id-only reconciliation pass that detects deletions and permission changes without refetching bodies, and per-document `ConnectorFailure`. Mastra's transactional **semantic outbox** for store↔index consistency, which belongs in `ef`→`vd` rather than in kc. LangGraph's per-write index control (`index: false | string[]`) — the difference between a store that can hold confidential material and one that leaks it into a vector index by default. Atlas's classification propagation along lineage edges, so marking a source "restricted" flows automatically to every chunk, embedding and answer derived from it. SHACL shapes as the thing that turns "attributable" from an aspiration into a test that can fail. And the eval vocabulary, which costs nothing and is worth more than any corpus: **Info Not Found / Conflicting Info / Completeness** from EnterpriseRAG-Bench [5], unanswerable turns from MTRAG and TechQA [8], knowledge-updates from LongMemEval, CRAG's −1/0/+1 scoring that makes *missing* strictly better than *incorrect*, FreshStack's nugget-level qrels for per-deployment benchmarking on corpora that cannot be redistributed [7], and SARA's relevance-crossed-with-sensitivity design — because every other benchmark in the field assumes the searcher may see the whole corpus, which is false in every real organisation, and a system that scores perfectly on EnterpriseRAG-Bench can still be a data-leak incident.

## The facade question

The profile table in `docs/notes-whitepaper.md` was written from vendor material. Every row has now been checked against primary sources at fixed commits — source read, not docs summarised: [mem0](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0) at `mem0ai` 2.0.19 (HEAD 19cb89a, 2026-08-28), [Graphiti](https://github.com/getzep/graphiti) at `graphiti-core` 0.29.3 (HEAD 8b61fce, 2026-08-30), [Letta](https://github.com/letta-ai/letta) plus [letta-code](https://github.com/letta-ai/letta-code) and the Cloud SDK, the DIY Markdown-and-git pattern against Claude Code v2.1.251 and its published loading rules, and Gorgias Cortex against six first-party engineering write-ups. Two framing notes. **Two of the five are libraries; three have no library at all; and none of the five is a component at the grain a facade needs.** A component does one job and lets you supply the rest. mem0 and Graphiti are importable, but neither lets you take its ranker without its extractor, or its extractor without its store — mem0 *owns* the embedder and the store choice, Graphiti *owns* the graph schema and the write-path model budget. The other three cannot be imported under any circumstances: Zep and Cortex are services, and Letta's state layer stopped being self-hostable software the day the server was deleted. The genuine components in this space live one layer down — embedders, vector stores, rerankers, BM25, a graph DB — which is exactly the grain `vd` and `ef` already face.

That does most of the damage to the facade hypothesis before any API design begins. A facade abstracts over interchangeable *implementations*; here there are five products, at three different altitudes, of which three are not obtainable. The published papers make it worse rather than better: mem0's own paper describes the v2 adjudication loop the code no longer runs [21], Graphiti's paper describes a reflexion pass absent at 0.29.3 [19], and Letta's describes heartbeats retired in the default agent type [20]. Even the primary literature is profiling frames, not systems.

### Does a facade over these make sense?

Start with why a vector-database facade works, because the contrast is the whole argument. Every `vd` backend implements the **same primitive** — approximate nearest neighbour over a fixed-dimension vector under a named distance metric — and differs only in syntax: how you construct a client, how you spell a filter, whether the metric is called `cosine` or `COSINE`. Qdrant and pgvector disagree about vocabulary and agree about meaning, so a translation layer is lossless and a caller can swap backends without rewriting a question. The facade is a dialect translator over one shared semantic.

These five agree about nothing at that level, starting with what a memory *is*.

| System | The stored unit | Write semantics | What an empty result means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mem0** | A 15–80-word English sentence extracted by an LLM, carrying `attributed_to` ∈ {user, assistant} | A **proposal**: one LLM call that may legitimately store nothing — unless `infer=False`, which is a genuine verbatim put | Nothing cleared the semantic pre-gate (threshold 0.1, applied *before* BM25 and entity can rescue a candidate) |
| **Graphiti** | A typed edge between typed entities carrying four timestamps on two independent axes | Extraction, dedup, contradiction adjudication and validity extraction, at O(entities + edges) model calls | Nothing scored, in this `group_id` — and invalidated edges were included, unless you filtered them out |
| **Letta** | A reserved segment of the system prompt with a character limit, a label, a description and a `read_only` flag | A literal put — but behaviourally inert until the next prompt compile | A category error: a block is never retrieved, it is always present |
| **DIY** | A lexical span at `path:line` | Two native outcomes: a file write (lands verbatim) or a pull request (resolves next Tuesday, or never) | Your words are not the corpus's words — which is not the same fact as "nothing scored well" |
| **Cortex** | A row in a generated BigQuery table, or a Markdown node carrying `owner: email` that routes a human review | Derived: the index is a build artifact regenerated wholesale from dbt YAML and Markdown in CI; the writable object is the source file | Often nothing, because the majority read path was a *compiler* — the agent writes SQL |

Each of these also bundles its own embedder, its own store and its own extraction policy, none of which is separable. So a facade over whole systems has exactly two shapes available, and both fail.

**The union** carries every argument every backend needs: `infer`, `occurred_at`/`reference_time` (required positional on Graphiti, meaningless on Letta), `group_id`, an agent identity plus a model plus an embedding model (Letta's non-negotiable price of a memory container), a path (DIY), a department and an owner email (Cortex), plus a capability set that must be keyed on **backend × tier × surface** — because mem0's `decay` is a licensing boundary rather than an architectural one, and Graphiti's MCP surface exposes only the event-time half of its own bi-temporal model, so the same system has different temporal capability depending on how you reach it. That is not a facade. It is a dispatch table with five type systems glued to it, and the caller still has to know which backend they are on to fill it in.

**The intersection** is store text / retrieve text. That is an existing package plus a dict — `ef` for the retrieval half and a `MutableMapping` for the other. There is no product in it, and it is not even semantically uniform: the empty-result column above shows one return type carrying five incompatible meanings, and the whitepaper's own non-negotiable — silent degradation must never be permitted — is violated *inside the intersection*, by Letta's own local backend, in a source comment. Flatten the record to `.text` and you additionally destroy the `owner: email` that routes Cortex's only human loop, the `attributed_to` that tells an LLM's inference from a person's statement, and the character limit that makes a Letta block a prompt segment rather than a passage.

Combine that with the component-level verdict already reached in the notes, which the verification sharpens rather than overturns.

| Component | Verdict | What the primary sources add |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture** | **YES — as a verb, with two paths** | The distil-vs-verbatim split is not a design preference, it is a *runtime* seam already exposed by the systems: mem0's `infer=False` is a real put with zero model calls, Letta's block write is a put, DIY has both a put and a PR. So the honest surface is a lossy `ingest` returning a receipt **and** a verbatim `put`, with the backend declaring which it supports — plus `Applied` vs `Proposed(ref=…)` as a return *type*, because DIY is the backend where both outcomes are native and the caller cannot infer which they got from the arguments. The receipt must also report model calls: a $0.002 mem0 `add()` and a Graphiti episode whose fan-out scales with its own content must not look like the same method |
| **Retrieval** | **YES — as a verb, with capability enforced at the boundary** | The capability set cannot be advisory metadata; it must be checkable and must be able to fail the call, because a shipped system already degrades silently and mem0 already demonstrates the correct alternative (a `ValueError` naming the parameter and the tier). Records need `kind` and `provenance`, and — grep forces this — a `match_type`, because rank-by-match-order is not a relevance score. Coverage must be a channel distinct from relevance: absence is the fact a uniform return type cannot express |
| **Representation** | **NO — declare, never unify** | Confirmed for a reason the notes did not anticipate: a Letta block is not a retrievable record at all, so the capability vocabulary needs `unit=context_segment` alongside `unit=retrieved_passage`. `supports_temporal` is likewise the wrong shape — Graphiti has two axes and one vendor exposes different subsets on different surfaces, so it must be `temporal_axes={event, belief}` per surface, with an as-of on the wrong axis raising rather than answering the other one |
| **Maintenance** | **NO — and `prune()` would be worse than no facade** | The same call is: nothing at all on DIY, where no expiry field exists to set; enqueued work for a *second LLM agent* on Letta; a pull request on Cortex; a real `DETACH DELETE` on Graphiti that exists at four granularities and never fires on its own; and ordinary CRUD always returning `Applied` on mem0. One name, five semantics, latency from microseconds to never, no guarantee the write lands. The policy object needs two orthogonal fields — *what happens automatically* and *what erasure is expressible* — because collapsing Graphiti to "append-only" tells a GDPR-motivated caller the opposite of the truth. Cortex is the confirmation: a production system independently converged on findings → clustered → PR, which is a registry, not a method |

**The verdict: a facade over whole brain systems is the wrong shape, but a narrower facade over two of the four components is right — and the two are capture and retrieval.** They are the two that generalise because they are *verbs*: writing something down and asking for it back are operations every one of these systems performs, and the differences between their performances are expressible as declared arguments and declared capabilities. Representation and maintenance do not generalise because they are *nouns and policies*: what a memory is, and who owns truth. There is no argument you can pass to make a prompt segment into a graph edge, and no return type that makes "I deleted it" and "I opened a pull request about it" the same event. Face capture and retrieval; face representation's read path only as a capability declaration with a documented escape hatch to native calls; model maintenance as a registry of independently-named policies that report findings, in the shape this repo's own `align`/`upkeep` checks already take.

One consequence worth stating plainly, because it changes what an adapter costs. Only two of the five can be adapted as libraries at all. A mem0 adapter inherits an embedder, a store, an extraction prompt and a phone-home; a Graphiti adapter inherits a graph database to operate and a write bill proportional to content; a Letta adapter wraps *an agent*, not a store, and arrives with a model and an embedding choice attached; Cortex and Zep can only be reached over the network, if at all. The facade is thin not because the design is elegant but because the surface area anyone can honestly claim to span is genuinely small.

### (b) Dependency direction: BESIDE — never below, never above

The test the survey applies is one sentence: **a correct position for a new package requires no published package to change.** Three positions were argued seriously; only one passes it.

The proposed edges, concretely:

| Edge | Kind | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `kc → dol` | base | Persistence is free and reversible via injection; inventing it is neither |
| `kc → config2py` | base | Every connector needs a secret and an XDG-correct directory |
| `kc → lkj` | base | Zero-dep, arrives transitively anyway; stops kc growing a `util.py` |
| `kc → i2` | base | `castgraph` is the "stable role, unstable representation" machine for knowledge references |
| `kc → aix` | base | Every LLM call. **Never `oa`** — joining oa's dependent list moves kc backwards against fleet policy |
| `kc → py2mcp` | base | The MCP surface is ≈ one `mk_mcp_from_refs` call, with `middleware=` as the enforcement chokepoint |
| `kc[web] → graze` | extra | Web fetch + cache |
| `kc[github] → hubcap` | extra | Cheapest correct GitHub connector — but it drags **pandas**, so never a base dep |
| `ef`, `vd` | **transitive only, through `ir`** | A direct `kc → vd` edge is *evidence kc is redoing ef's or ir's job* and should be treated as a design failure signal, not a convenience |
| `coact → kc` | inbound, later | `AgentDefinition.memory` is today a host scope literal (`'user'|'project'|'local'`) with no memory system behind it; "which knowledge context does this agent read, under what attribution rules" is an `AgentDefinition` field. coact can add this as one keyword argument without kc's involvement |
| `opsward → kc` | inbound, later | It already routes cross-repo asset search to `toolery` |

Nothing else changes. The seam kc plugs into is already open and costs nothing to use: a raglab source is any `callable(query, **params) -> Sequence[ir.SearchHit]` registered in a `Mapping[name, Retriever]`, and RRF fan-in already handles incommensurable cross-source scores — so **policy-carrying, attributed connectors plug into `make_search_agent(sources)` with zero changes to raglab or ir**.

What the rejected positions would cost, package by package:

| Position | Package that must change | What breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **BELOW** | `dol` | Declares `dependencies = []` on purpose and gates PRs on an 89-dependent test ledger. Nothing about kc may enter dol — forbidden outright |
| **BELOW** | `ef` | Its written boundary (`.claude/CLAUDE.md` §6) forbids absorbing orchestration; taking an upward-facing dependency violates it |
| **BELOW** | `ir` | v0.1.29, 3 consumers, deliberately offline-by-default dependency set; would gain a heavy upward dependency and stop being what it is |
| **BELOW** | `vd` | Would gain its first non-pyyaml dependency |
| **ABOVE** | `raglab` | Would have to cede the control loop — or kc duplicates Planner/Formulator/Evaluator and the back-edge wholesale. There is no room above raglab for a second loop |
| **ABOVE** | `ir` | Would have to cede federation — or kc duplicates `ir.discover`. And `toolery` already occupies the remaining slot (uniform attributed `Card` + pluggable search + MCP) with the exact record shape kc claims |

BELOW fails the test four times over; ABOVE fails it twice, and both ABOVE failures are "duplicate an assembly that already exists" — because the assembly exists **twice**: `ir` *is* the assembly of ef+vd with a corpus contract on top (that is what its 19k LOC are), and `raglab` *is* the assembly of ir with a control loop on top, policed one-way ("raglab imports ir; ir must NEVER import raglab").

**Verdict: BESIDE, and only if the access-control contract is real.**

### (c) The gaps, ranked — and the gap is small

Decomposing what mem0 / Zep-Graphiti / Letta actually are and checking each component against the fleet leaves six uncovered items. **One is unclaimed. Say so plainly rather than dressing up the other five.**

| # | Gap | Status | Where it belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Access control / permission-aware retrieval** | **Unclaimed — the only one** | Plausibly kc |
| 2 | **Write/consolidation semantics with bi-temporal validity** | Claimed by an open issue | `raglab#6` (PurposeStore), ADR'd and specced |
| 3 | **Shared, multi-user storage for the retrieval substrate** | Claimed by an open issue, explicitly deferred | `ir#28` |
| 4 | **Fetch provenance** (ETag, Last-Modified, `fetched_at`, content-type) | Unclaimed but misplaced in kc | Upstream in `graze`, as a sidecar store keyed by the same cache key |
| 5 | **Connectors to org systems** (Slack, Jira, Confluence, code hosts) | Not a gap — a to-do list | Each is one `CorpusSource` classmethod (~40 lines, cf. `from_md_reports`); normalisation already lives in `contaix` |
| 6 | **Async** | A decision, not a build | Decide the seam explicitly now |

**1. Access control — the strongest, and the only genuinely unclaimed one.** A word-boundary grep across `ir`, `raglab` and `contaix` for `acl` / `permission` / `authoriz` / `tenant` / `principal` / `entitle` / `redact` / `actor` returns **nothing** — verified; the only near-hit is the word "auditable" in `ir.select`, and it is about score provenance. "What an organisation knows" means *different answers for different askers*, and authorization is not a feature you add later: it must be enforced at the retriever/store boundary or every facade above it leaks. The seam is already load-bearing and free — `ir`'s `metadata_of` stamps arbitrary `filter_fields`, and `filter` is a vd Mongo-style **hard pre-filter applied to the candidate set before ranking in dense, lexical and hybrid alike**. `lacing.ArtifactStore`'s docstring is explicit that access calculus is deliberately absent and belongs to a wrapping facade; **kc is plausibly that facade.** This is a *filter-field convention plus an enforcement wrapper*, not a second retrieval engine.

**2. Write/consolidation semantics — real, but claimed by name.** [thorwhalen/raglab#6](https://github.com/thorwhalen/raglab/issues/6) ("PurposeStore — persistent purpose-indexed memory overlay") is verified OPEN: decision 6 of an ADR, maintainer-confirmed, specced to a stage-0 acceptance criterion (*"the same purpose run twice reuses prior extractions instead of re-sweeping"*), with an `ExtractionUnit` keyed by (purpose, source-unit) carrying provenance pointers and confidence, a RAW→SYNOPSIS→INSIGHT ladder, `supersedes`, purpose embeddings, a purpose DAG, and write/read/consolidate/decay as injected Protocols. It explicitly names *"Zep-style SUPERSEDE + `source_valid_at` bi-temporal staleness"* as a staged follow-up — **as a native dol+vd implementation, deliberately not a facade over Zep.** Its justification is architectural and hard to argue with: **raglab is the only layer that sees purpose** (`Query`, `SubTask.goal`, `Judgement`, the refinement chain), so a purpose-indexed overlay must live where purpose is observable. A kc that builds memory elsewhere either duplicates that or needs raglab to hand it the purpose signal — the same coupling with an extra package in it. **Whichever way this goes, close the other; running both is how two memory layers start disagreeing.**

**3. Shared storage — real, blocking, and someone else's issue.** `ir/policy.py` pins `STORAGE_BACKENDS = ('local',)` — verified in source. Unifying storage behind `vd.Collection` is [i2mint/ir#28](https://github.com/i2mint/ir/issues/28), verified OPEN and explicitly deferred. This is precisely what makes `ir` a personal tool rather than an organisational one. Fixing it *there* turns ir into a shareable layer with **no new package**; leaving it open means kc inherits the same wall.

**4. Fetch provenance — small, and belongs upstream.** Build the fetch-metadata sidecar in `graze`: every graze consumer wants it, and a kc-side copy would cover only the URLs that happened to arrive through kc.

**5. Connectors — a to-do list, not a package.** `ir`'s five `CorpusSource` presets are local-filesystem or priv-derived; GitHub issues/PRs were named as a target and never built, though `hubcap` already has them. That is "an ingestion story on top", not a distribution.

The honest summary: of six gaps, **one is unclaimed, two are claimed by open issues in packages that already exist, one belongs upstream in `graze`, one is a connector to-do list across `contaix`/`ir`/`hubcap`, and one is a decision.** A package whose entire uncovered contribution is an access-control contract plus a freshness envelope is a thin package, not a platform — and an access-control *contract* (a filter-field convention plus an enforcement wrapper at the retriever boundary) may be better landed as a shared type that `ir`, `contaix`, `hubcap` and `dn` all emit than as a layer wrapped around them. Note also that [i2mint/dol#80](https://github.com/i2mint/dol/issues/80) is likewise verified OPEN: the record/query layer is designed and tracked, so building it in kc is duplication of a *planned* feature, not colonisation of empty ground.

### (d) The cautionary in-fleet precedent: `ayn`

`ayn` attempted exactly the facade shape kc is contemplating, one domain over: "one facade over many third-party agent systems" — search GitHub/HuggingFace/awesome-lists for agents, wrap CrewAI/LangChain/AutoGen behind one controller, export as FastAPI/LangServe/MCP/ChatGPT-action. Verified state: **no `tests/` directory at all** (pytest is configured `--doctest-modules` over `ayn/`, so its "20 passed" is doctests of dataclasses); `GitHubAgentSearcher.search` returns `[]` behind a `# TODO`; `HuggingFaceAgentSearcher.search` is `try/pass/return []`; `AwesomeListSearcher.search` returns `[]` behind a TODO; `CrewAIController`, `LangChainController` and `AutoGenController` are literally `pass`; its `MCPServer` is a hand-rolled list of dicts bound to no transport and the `mcp` extra is empty; **the README's own Quick Start raises `IndexError` on step 2**; zero consumers; and a 13KB `FUTURE_DEVELOPMENT.md` of features never built.

What distinguishes a facade that earned its shape from one that did not — and it is not vision, because ayn's pitch reads as well as vd's:

| Property | `vd` (earned) | `ayn` (did not) |
|---|---|---|
| **Do the backends implement the same primitive?** | Yes — ~15 systems all do approximate nearest neighbour over a vector and disagree only on syntax. That prior agreement is what let vd *name* three portable contracts: filter AST, score scale, RRF fusion at k=60 | No — CrewAI, LangChain and AutoGen do not agree on what an agent is |
| **Does a real consumer call through the facade?** | Yes — `ef` and `ir` genuinely call `vd`, in production, before adapter #15 existed | No — zero consumers, ever |
| **Do the adapters connect?** | Yes — a parametrized `client` fixture runs one assertion across every embedded backend; unreachable servers skip *with a reason* rather than failing | No — every searcher returns `[]`, every controller is `pass` |
| **Is "cannot do that" a vocabulary or a shrug?** | Three states: unknown name (`ValueError` listing what is registered) / known-but-uninstalled (`BackendNotInstalledError` carrying the exact pip line) / registered-but-unsupported (a typed capability error) | No taxonomy |
| **Are capability differences negotiated or flattened?** | Four mechanisms chosen per axis: narrowed vocabulary validated centrally *before* dispatch; client-side emulation against one reference semantics; `@runtime_checkable` Protocols where there is no syntactic convergence; a boolean flag where the caller must branch before an error could exist | None |
| **Is there an escape hatch?** | `client.client` and `collection.native`, documented as supported API — "the facade never traps you" | n/a |
| **Is the descriptive layer honest about what has no adapter?** | `providers.yaml` covers ~21 systems against 14 adapters and reports `has_adapter: False` for seven of them | The README implies coverage that does not exist |

Two further in-fleet data points sharpen the same edge. **`coact`** is real, well-argued, best-documented in its group — and has zero consumers; its realization backends return genuinely heterogeneous types (`RealizedHost`, `RunnableAgent`, `RunnableLLMAgent`, `RunnableLLMGraphAgent`, `RunnableCrewAIAgent`, `Any`) and `realize()` is honestly typed `-> Any`. That is defensible for coact and **fatal for kc**: if `kc.connect('mem0')` and `kc.connect('graphiti')` return objects of different shape, kc is a naming convention, not a facade. And **`aix` is not the precedent it looks like** — it is not an N-adapter registry at all; it has exactly one backend, litellm, which is itself the aggregator, and aix's contribution is ergonomics plus a layered config SSOT plus lazy-import discipline. **There is no litellm for memory systems.** So "kc : brains :: aix : LLM providers" is not available; kc-as-facade means paying vd's bill (N adapters, capability negotiation, an error taxonomy, per-backend extras, a `kc-add-backend` maintenance discipline), not aix's cheap one.

And the disanalogy that may forbid the shape outright: mem0, Zep/Graphiti and Letta **do not agree on what a memory is** — mem0 is an LLM-extracted fact store with conflict resolution; Graphiti/Zep is a bi-temporal episodic knowledge graph with edge invalidation over time; Letta is agent-resident context-block management. Each also *bundles* an embedder, a vector store, often a graph store and an opinionated extraction policy — i.e. each is an **application**, and vd's own prior-art notes record the txtai lesson that bundling the DB makes it an app, not a facade. A facade over them exposes either their union (not a facade) or their intersection — `store text`, `retrieve text`, which is `ir` plus a dict. **Before committing to the facade shape, name the single operation all three implement, with its identity model, its time model and its attribution model. If that cannot be written down concretely, the facade collapses to lowest common denominator — the exact failure vd's complexity exists to avoid.**

### (f) The strongest argument that kc should not exist

**State it at full strength.** Every component of a knowledge-context layer is already built, tested and — for the ones that matter — running in production in this fleet. Retrieval, federation, attribution identity, abstention, progressive disclosure, graph traversal, coverage diagnostics: `ir`, 19.3k LOC, 430 tests, in daily use. Ingest, embedding, content-addressed invalidation, staleness, refresh: `ef`. Vector storage across ~15 backends with the three portable contracts that make fusion work at all: `vd`. The agentic loop above it: `raglab`, with a documented one-way boundary. The uniform attributed catalog with a pluggable search backend and an MCP surface — **kc's stated unit and kc's stated shape** — is already shipped as `toolery.Card` / `Catalog`. The provenance vocabulary is `lacing`, PROV-O and W3C-Web-Annotation-shaped, live in production. Chat capture with egress scrubbing and per-record freshness verification is `openloops`, in daily use. The MCP surface with a per-call audit chokepoint is `py2mcp`, 14 consumers. The record/query layer is *designed and tracked* as dol#80. Of the six things left over, two are claimed by open issues in packages that already exist (raglab#6, ir#28), one belongs upstream in graze, one is a forty-line-per-connector to-do list, and one is a decision about async. **That leaves exactly one unclaimed capability — access control — and it is a filter-field convention plus an enforcement wrapper over a pre-filter seam `ir` already applies before ranking in all three modes.** A convention plus a wrapper is a module. The fleet also contains a fresh, verified example of what happens when this argument is ignored: `ayn`, structurally identical to the kc hypothesis one domain over, is now unimplemented controllers, searchers that return `[]`, zero consumers, and a README whose own quick start raises `IndexError`. And it contains a documented precedent for choosing *not* to build (openloops-lab's verdict), whose surviving rule is ADR-013: **if you cannot state in one sentence which existing daily habit the thing displaces, do not build it.** kc lands in two of the three exceptions that raise the bar further — it cannot be cheaply removed (it is already a published PyPI distribution at 0.0.8, so deletion is partly someone else's cost) and its failure mode is silent (a retrieval layer that answers "nothing relevant" is the falsely-clean-slate failure the risk register calls *worse than no ledger at all*). One more datum should trouble anyone scoping this: the fleet's last capture-discipline problem — a verified 100% miss rate, 8 of 8 sessions — **was fixed with prose, not with a store**: one skill plus one line in the global instructions, and the metric moved.

**The strongest rebuttal.** Access control is not a feature; it is a *position*. Authorization enforced anywhere other than the retriever/store boundary leaks through every facade above it, which means it cannot be retrofitted into `ir` without `ir` becoming something it has declared it is not, and it cannot be bolted onto four layers independently without four of them disagreeing. The one thing in this fleet that no existing package can own — and that no open issue claims — is the *chokepoint*: a place where a principal, a policy and an attribution stamp are applied once, at the boundary where they cannot be bypassed, and below which every retrieval path already funnels. `lacing.ArtifactStore` says so explicitly in its own docstring: the access calculus is deliberately absent and belongs to a wrapping facade. Beside that, `coact.AgentDefinition.memory` is a declared hole with a host-scope literal and nothing behind it — "which knowledge context does this agent read, and under what attribution rules" is a field with no owner. And the fleet's assembled pieces are demonstrably personal rather than organisational for exactly one reason (`ir.policy.STORAGE_BACKENDS = ('local',)`), so the thin-sounding pair *access contract + shared addressability* is precisely the pair that converts a very good personal retrieval stack into something an organisation can point at.

**Verdict.** The gap is real but narrow, and the honest framing is that **kc's defensible v1 is a contract, not a platform** — a filter-field convention plus an enforcement wrapper at the retriever boundary, carrying a freshness envelope, positioned BESIDE the stack and requiring no published package to change. If kc adds adapters over mem0/Zep/Graphiti before that contract is written down and consumed by something real, it becomes `ayn`; vd's own history says the descriptive-registry layer (~900 lines plus a data file, independently useful, what the agent skills actually consulted) came *first* and the adapters came *last*. Three preconditions should gate the first commit, and none of them is code: name the single operation mem0/Zep/Letta all implement with its identity, time and attribution models; decide the raglab#6 question and **close the loser**; and write the kill criterion, with deletion as one documented command, before the first feature ships. If those three cannot be answered, the correct outcome is not a smaller kc — it is an access-control type emitted by `ir`, `contaix`, `hubcap` and `dn`, plus a patch to ir#28, and no new package at all.
